Metacognitive Governance and the Second Calibration Loop

THEMIS currently governs the machine and the human-machine trust boundary. It does not yet govern the analyst's model of themselves. This addendum specifies six capabilities that extend the governance layer from trust calibration to self-evaluation calibration, grounded in the Maier (2026) eight-axis framework and the Fernandes (2026) performance-metacognition decoupling result.

E.0 · Background and Motivation

Performance and metacognition can move in opposite directions

Recent literature establishes a finding with direct consequences for any platform that embeds AI assistance in analytic work: AI assistance can improve task performance while degrading the user's ability to judge their own performance. Fernandes et al. (2026) demonstrate this decoupling empirically in reasoning tasks. Liebenow et al. (2025) show that LLM-generated feedback does not, on average, improve self-assessment accuracy. The OECD (2026) names the policy-level risk a "mirage of false mastery."

Maier (2026) organizes this literature into an eight-axis explanatory framework: the functional position of AI in the task, reflective support versus cognitive substitution, metacognitive engagement, effort redistribution, cognitive engagement depth, the gap between assisted performance and actual learning, trust regulation and attribution of success, and self-evaluation accuracy. The framework's terminal construct is self-evaluation error:

Maier Axis 8 · Self-evaluation error EAE = |Jperf − Pactual|   ·   AAE ↓ ⇔ EAE ↑

Where Jperf is the analyst's judgment of their own performance and Pactual is performance against an independently verifiable criterion. Overestimation when Jperf > Pactual; underestimation when Jperf < Pactual.

For an intelligence production environment the stakes exceed the pedagogical framing of the source literature. An analyst who cannot accurately distinguish their own contribution from the system's contribution cannot accurately assess the confidence that should attach to a finished product, cannot reliably brief the provenance of a judgment, and may not retain the unassisted competence the mission requires under AI denial. Miscalibrated self-evaluation is therefore a mission assurance problem, not only a learning problem.

E.0.1 The gap in the current architecture

THEMIS closes one calibration loop today. MIMIR baselines model capability pre-deployment through Elo evaluation. The Reliance-Accuracy Index (RAI) measures whether analyst reliance on the system tracks the system's demonstrated accuracy at runtime. ATHENA delivers interventions when reliance and accuracy diverge. This is the trust loop: it calibrates the analyst's model of the AI.

Nothing in the current architecture calibrates the analyst's model of the analyst. The Fernandes result shows these are separate loops that can diverge: reliance on the system can be perfectly calibrated while self-assessment drifts into overestimation, because the analyst correctly trusts a capable system and then erroneously attributes the resulting success to their own competence. Maier's Axis 7 names this mechanism directly: erroneous attribution rises when the system's contribution is opaque, control is delegated, and independent verification is weak.

Capability Additions

Six capabilities to close the self loop

Each capability is specified against the Maier axis it operationalizes, the THEMIS services it touches, and the telemetry it consumes. All six respect the standing architectural rule: every tool call routes through ContextForge, and all observability flows through Langfuse into THEMIS.

E.1

Substitution Index (SXI)

Operationalizes Maier Axes 2, 3, 5 · Reflective support vs cognitive substitution

A per-session behavioral index distinguishing reflective use from substitutive use, computed entirely from telemetry THEMIS already collects. No new instrumentation is required; SXI is a computation over the existing ContextForge and Langfuse stream.

  • Time-to-accept: latency between output delivery and acceptance. Near-instant acceptance of substantive analytic content is a substitution signature.
  • Edit distance: normalized delta between generated content and content committed to the product. Zero-edit adoption at scale indicates substitution.
  • Verification actions: count and depth of source checks, cross-references, and tool calls that test rather than extend the generated claim.
  • Alternative comparison: whether the analyst requested or constructed competing formulations before committing.
Session-level signal · Maier Axis 2 restated over telemetry SXI ↑ ⇔ t_accept ↓, edit_dist ↓, verify_actions ↓, alt_compare ↓
ConsumesContextForge call log · Langfuse traces · product diffs
FeedsATHENA (intervention trigger) · ARGUS (aggregate drift)
New servicesNone. Computation added to existing RAI pipeline

E.2

Attribution Probes

Operationalizes Maier Axis 7 · Erroneous attribution of success

Lightweight post-task elicitations delivered through ATHENA, sampled rather than universal to limit friction. Probes ask the analyst to delimit contribution: which conclusions in this product could you defend without the system, which claims did you independently verify, which elements were generated and adopted. Responses are scored against the cognitive provenance ledger (E.3) once it exists; until then they serve as self-report data and as labeled training signal for SXI thresholds.

Maier Axis 7 · Attribution risk RAE ↑ ⇔ TAI > FAI, CAI ↑, OAI ↑, Vind ↓
ConsumesSession product · SXI · provenance ledger (E.3, later)
FeedsEAE loop (E.5) · SXI label set · ARGUS
New servicesNone. Intervention catalog entries 32 through 34
SamplingStratified by SXI band; oversample high-substitution sessions
E.3

Cognitive Provenance Ledger

Operationalizes Maier Axes 1, 7 · Process transparency and contribution delimitation

ARBOR's cryptographic chain of custody answers "where did this evidence come from and who touched it." The cognitive provenance ledger extends the same discipline to analytic authorship: for every section of a finished product, a queryable record of whether it was AI-generated, AI-revised, human-authored, or human-verified, and in what order. Maier's central Axis 7 risk, the system's contribution being absorbed into an undifferentiated sense of success, is mitigated by making the contribution split a first-class queryable artifact rather than a memory.

The ledger also changes review economics. Two textually identical products are not epistemically identical: one that is 90 percent generated with zero verification events should present differently to a reviewer than one with heavy human revision and a dense verification trail. Review workflows gain a provenance panel; briefing workflows gain an honest answer to "how was this produced."

ConsumesContextForge stream · editor events · verification tool calls
FeedsReview UX · attribution probe scoring · SAGA session narrative
HomeALETHEIA (truth and disclosure), with SAGA rendering the narrative view
Schema impactExtends Mission Common Schema with an authorship-event type; one-directional flow into product metadata
E.4

Phase-Aware Capability Policy

Operationalizes Maier Axis 1 · Functional position of AI in the task

MOIRAI bounds what a session may do. This capability adds when: capability grants keyed to workflow phase, enforced by KAIROS, evaluated at ContextForge. The canonical policy is commit-then-assist: for designated task classes, generative drafting is locked until the analyst records an initial independent assessment. On unlock, the initial assessment is preserved immutably for comparison, giving the analyst (and E.2 probes) a before-and-after view of their own position.

This converts Maier's strongest practical recommendation, independent-first workflows, from a design guideline into an enforceable policy. It is precisely the kind of control only a governance layer at THEMIS's position in the stack can implement: it requires visibility across MOIRAI grants, KAIROS timing, and every tool call in the session.

  • Phase gates are per task class, not global. Routine formatting tasks need no gate; initial analytic judgment on priority intelligence requirements does.
  • The initial assessment is a governance artifact. Immutable, timestamped, joined to the final product in the provenance ledger.
  • Unlock is automatic on commit, never adjudicated by a human in the loop, to keep friction predictable and low.
Enforced byKAIROS (phase) · MOIRAI (grant) · ContextForge (evaluation)
FeedsProvenance ledger (E.3) · EAE loop (E.5)
New servicesNone. Policy vocabulary extension to MOIRAI grants
RiskAnalyst friction if task classes are drawn too broadly; start narrow, expand on evidence
E.5

Self-Assessment Accuracy Loop (EAE)

Operationalizes Maier Axis 8 · The terminal construct

The third runtime metric of the calibration architecture, alongside MIMIR Elo and RAI. At sampled decision points ATHENA elicits the analyst's confidence in their own judgment, explicitly distinct from their confidence in the system's output. Where outcome ground truth becomes available, judged performance is scored against actual performance, producing a running EAE per analyst, per task class, per assistance configuration.

Runtime metric · directly implements Maier Axis 8 EAE = |Jperf − Pactual|  ·  tracked per analyst × task class × assist config

This is genuinely additive to the dual-metric architecture, not a refinement of it. RAI asks: does reliance track system accuracy? EAE asks: does self-assessment track own accuracy? The intervention logic differs accordingly. RAI divergence triggers trust-side interventions; EAE divergence triggers attribution-side interventions (E.2 probes, provenance surfacing, phase-gate tightening).

Elicitation surfaceATHENA · sampled, low-friction, decision-point anchored
Scoring homeTrust Calibration Service, alongside RAI
Ground truthAdjudicated outcomes, red-team review, downstream validation events; sparse and lagged, treated as such
Dissertation tieDirect operationalization of the Maier framework; see E.9
E.6

METIS: Unassisted Competence Baselining

Operationalizes Maier Axis 6 · Assisted performance vs actual learning

The largest structural addition, and the only one proposed as a named service. MIMIR baselines models; nothing baselines analysts. Maier's Axis 6 gap is only measurable if actual unassisted competence is periodically measured:

Maier Axis 6 · Vulnerability of self-evaluation VAE ↑ ⇔ |P_AI − L_actual| ↑

METIS (proposed name: mother of Athena, personification of practical wisdom, a deliberate pairing with the ATHENA intervention surface) schedules and scores periodic AI-off tasks that establish whether independent competence is holding, growing, or decaying under sustained assistance. Task instances draw from the same classes as assisted work so that P_AI and L_actual are commensurable.

The domain justification exceeds the pedagogical one. In a contested or airgapped scenario the platform may degrade or be denied. Whether analysts can still perform is a continuity-of-operations question. METIS reframes skill decay monitoring as a readiness metric, which is also the framing most likely to secure the organizational buy-in this capability requires.

New serviceMETIS (proposed) · schedules, administers, scores AI-off tasks
FeedsEAE ground truth · Axis 6 gap metric · readiness reporting
DependenciesOrganizational policy for protected AI-off time; task bank per class
SensitivityIndividual results are calibration data, not performance evaluation; ARGUS aggregates, LOGOS governs access. This boundary must be explicit in policy or the telemetry will be gamed

Design note

Axis 4 (effort redistribution) is deliberately left without a dedicated capability. Measuring where saved effort goes requires task-time accounting that is either invasive or unreliable. The bet is that SXI plus EAE capture its downstream effects; revisit if EAE trends cannot be explained by the covered axes.

E.9 · Research Crosswalk

Dissertation implications

Framing. Maier (2026) explicitly names the gap between trust-in-AI research and self-evaluation research, and the Trust Calibration Service sits astride exactly that seam. The paper supplies the published conceptual argument for why continuous calibration must include both loops. Her Axis 7 expression (RAE ↑ ⇔ TAI > FAI, ...) is a qualitative restatement of what RAI measures quantitatively: the dissertation is already past the point where the paper stops, and should say so in the literature review.

Modality experiment. The scaffolding-density staircase (basic chat through full ATHENA, three rungs) is a near-direct operationalization of the Axis 1 and Axis 2 configuration space. Framing the rungs as points along the reflective-support-to-substitution continuum gives the experiment a published theoretical anchor and a citable vocabulary for the manipulation.

Publication opportunity. The paper's limitations section is a roadmap of what a conceptual review cannot do and an instrumented environment can: she calls for operationalization through indicators such as verification actions, justification capacity, and performance stability without support, all of which the ContextForge and Langfuse stream produces natively. A paper titled roughly "Operationalizing self-evaluation calibration in an instrumented analytic environment" is low marginal effort once E.1 and E.5 are live, and connects the Brunswik and Hammond lens-model work on cue utilization to a running system.

Construct hygiene. Keep RAI and EAE strictly separate in instruments and analysis. Eliciting "how confident are you in this answer" without specifying whose answer conflates the two loops and reproduces the confound the dual-loop model exists to resolve.
